There is a row of $n \geq 3$ icebergs, labelled from left to right with the integers $1$ to $n$.
Pingu starts dancing on one of the icebergs and then moves according to the following rules.

If Pingu has just danced on iceberg $k$,
he then makes a sequence of $k$ hops to an adjacent iceberg.
The first of the $k$ hops is always to the right, unless Pingu is one iceberg $n$,
in which case it is to the left.
Each of the subsequent $k-1$ hops is in the same direction as the previous hop,
unless Pingu is on iceberg $1$ or $n$ in which case he changes direction.
Pingu dances on the iceberg he lands on after $k$ hops.

Pingu continues hopping and dancing in this manner indefinitely.
An iceberg is \emph{inevitable} if Pingu eventually dances on it,
regardless of the iceberg he starts on.
Determine all $n \ge 3$ for which there is exactly one inevitable iceberg.

\emph{Example:} If $n = 8$, and Pingu first dances on iceberg $3$,
then the next four icebergs he dances on are $6, 4, 8$ and $2$, in this order.
